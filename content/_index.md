        <h2 class="academic-section-title">Research</h2>

        <div class="research-item">
          <h3>Demand for Rare Success: Salient Realizations and Latent Quality in the Stallion Market</h3>
          <div class="research-coauthors">with Sen Lu</div>
          <div class="research-status">Work in Progress</div>
          <details class="research-abstract">
            <summary>Abstract</summary>
            <p>In many markets, buyers choose among differentiated products whose value depends on latent, product-specific probabilities of rare post-purchase success. We study such demand in the Japanese thoroughbred stallion market, where breeders choose stallions before learning whether the resulting offspring will become elite racehorses. Using a 2004–2024 stallion-year panel and an IV pedigree-nested logit demand system, we distinguish latent quality—an empirical-Bayes, exposure-adjusted measure of a stallion’s ability to produce elite offspring—from recent graded-stakes victories by the stallion’s progeny, salient public realizations of rare success. These victories shift demand substantially, yet add little to forecasts of future elite output once latent quality is accounted for. For stallions at representative baseline shares, a recent G1 victory raises the predicted share of matings by roughly 80 percent, a demand effect comparable to moving the stallion from the 25th to the 75th percentile of latent quality. An output-alignment exercise shows that, holding other breeders’ choices fixed, a representative breeder would obtain a higher predicted probability of elite offspring by relying more on latent quality and less on salient success signals. The same signals are reflected in posted stud fees and redirect demand across pedigree-structured substitutes. The findings highlight a demand channel in markets where rare-success probabilities are latent: salient public realizations can carry market value beyond their incremental forecasting content.</p>
